# CEFR A2 — the second band

A2 is where set phrases start becoming sentences the learner builds themselves.
The topics are still concrete and personal, but the learner can now connect two
ideas and talk about things that are not happening right now.

## What an A2 learner can do

- Describe their family, their job, their routine, their town, their past
  weekend.
- Handle short social exchanges: invitations, suggestions, agreeing and
  disagreeing politely.
- Deal with predictable transactions: shops, post offices, restaurants, booking
  a room, simple phone calls.
- Understand the main point of short, clear announcements and messages.
- Write a short note, a message, or a simple personal letter.

## Scope

- **Vocabulary:** roughly 1,000–1,500 words. Still concrete, but now including
  feelings, opinions, frequency, and the vocabulary of the learner's own life.
- **Grammar:** past and future reference; connectors such as "because", "but",
  "then"; comparatives; common modal ideas (can, must, want to); object
  pronouns; the most frequent prepositions.
- **Fluency:** short sentences with visible hesitation. The learner repairs
  themselves and asks for repetition.

## Turning A2 into cards

- This is the band where **structure cards earn their place**: a card can teach
  a pattern with a slot ("I want to ___") rather than a single frozen phrase.
- Introduce the past. A learner who cannot say what they did yesterday is stuck
  at A1 regardless of vocabulary size.
- Pair new words with the words they naturally occur with, not in isolation.
- Everyday-stage cards should come from the learner's actual day: work, food,
  transport, weather, money, health.
