# CEFR B2 — arguing your case

B2 is where a learner becomes a full participant rather than a guest. They can
hold their own in discussion with native speakers without either side straining,
and can argue a position rather than just report facts.

## What a B2 learner can do

- Take an active part in discussion, explaining and defending a viewpoint.
- Understand extended speech, most news, and films in standard speech.
- Write clear, detailed text and explain the advantages and disadvantages of an
  option.
- Adjust register between formal and informal with reasonable success.

## Scope

- **Vocabulary:** roughly 4,000–6,000 words, with idiom, collocation and
  connotation mattering as much as count.
- **Grammar:** control rather than coverage. Errors remain but rarely cause
  misunderstanding.

## Turning B2 into cards

- Flashcards stop being the main tool here. What still works: idioms,
  collocations, and pairs of words the learner keeps confusing.
- Cards that ask for an **opinion spoken aloud** beat cards that ask for a
  translation.
