# CEFR A1 — the first band

A1 is the first level at which someone can be said to use the language at all.
The defining idea is **concrete, immediate need, in set phrases**. An A1 learner
handles very short exchanges about themselves and their surroundings, with a
patient speaker who repeats and rephrases.

## What an A1 learner can do

- Give and ask for personal details: name, where they live, where they are from,
  things they own, people they know.
- Use everyday greetings, farewells, please and thank you, and apologies.
- Ask for and give simple directions, prices, times and quantities.
- Order food, buy a ticket, ask where something is.
- Fill in a form with name, nationality and address.
- Understand slow, clear speech with long pauses, on familiar topics.

## Scope

- **Vocabulary:** roughly 500–750 words, overwhelmingly concrete nouns, a small
  set of high-frequency verbs, numbers, days, and fixed social phrases.
- **Grammar:** present tense of the most common verbs; simple negation; simple
  questions; basic word order; singular and plural; the most common pronouns and
  possessives. Whole phrases are often learned as single units rather than
  assembled from rules.
- **Pronunciation:** the learner is understood with effort. The sound inventory
  of the language, and the contrasts that change meaning, belong here.

## Turning A1 into cards

- Favour **fixed phrases over isolated words** for the survival stage: a card
  that teaches "where is the station?" is worth more at A1 than one that teaches
  "station".
- Teach the sounds of the writing system before the vocabulary that uses them
  when the script is unfamiliar to the learner.
- Keep every card answerable **out loud in under three seconds**. A1 recall is
  slow, and a long answer is really several cards.
- Prefer words a learner will meet on their first day in the country over words
  that appear in a textbook's first chapter for historical reasons.
