# CEFR B1 — the independence line

B1 is the first band where a learner can cope on their own. They can deal with
most situations that come up while travelling, follow a conversation between
other people on a familiar topic, and keep talking when a word is missing by
going around it.

## What a B1 learner can do

- Handle unexpected situations: a missed train, a problem with a bill, a
  doctor's appointment.
- Tell a story, describe a plan, explain a decision and give reasons.
- Express agreement, doubt, regret and preference.
- Understand the main points of radio, television and clear standard speech on
  familiar matters.

## Scope

- **Vocabulary:** roughly 2,000–3,000 words, including abstract ideas the
  learner cares about, and the beginnings of register awareness.
- **Grammar:** reported speech; conditionals; the full range of common tenses;
  relative clauses; a working command of aspect where the language has it.
- **Strategy:** the learner paraphrases around gaps rather than stopping. This
  is the real marker of B1.

## Turning B1 into cards

- Cards should carry **whole utterances with a function**, not vocabulary:
  "could you say that more slowly?" is a B1 survival skill.
- Real-talk cards belong here: contractions, fillers, and the fast forms of
  phrases the learner already knows in careful speech.
- Include repair phrases. A learner who can ask for help stays in the
  conversation.
