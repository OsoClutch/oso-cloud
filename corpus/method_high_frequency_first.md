# Choose words by what a learner will actually meet

Word frequency in every natural language follows a steep curve: a few hundred
words account for most of ordinary speech, and the gain per word falls away
sharply after the first few thousand. The first 1,000 words of a language are
worth more than the next 5,000 combined. So the selection question is not "what
is in chapter one" but **"what will this learner hit first"**.

## Two different kinds of frequency

- **Corpus frequency** — what is common in the language overall. Good for the
  everyday stage.
- **Situational frequency** — what is common in the situation the learner is
  about to be in. Good for the survival stage, and often very different: "excuse
  me", "how much", "I don't understand" and "where is the toilet" are not top-100
  corpus words, but they are the first things anyone needs.

A survival deck built from a frequency list alone is a bad survival deck.

## Consequence for generated decks

- `survival` cards: pick by situation — arrival, transport, food, money, asking
  for help, not understanding.
- `everyday` cards: pick by corpus frequency, concrete before abstract.
- Include the phrase that gets the learner **out of trouble**: how to say you
  don't understand, and how to ask someone to slow down. These are the highest
  value cards in any beginner deck and are routinely left out.
- Avoid words that are frequent only in textbooks, and avoid culturally specific
  items the learner cannot use on day one.
